# 2. Full Version — Extended Featured Article

## Same Heatpump. Why you pay more?

### One product, five countries — an expense gap that engineering cannot explain.

**HeatPump DataBase Europe Editorial Team | September 2026**

A four-person household in an existing house needs roughly 8 kW of heating with hot water. That requirement does not change at a border. This report asks what does.

We chose one product — **Daikin Altherma 3 R F, 8 kW, 230 L integrated cylinder**, Europe's highest-volume air/water heat pump, neither a budget import nor a premium line — and followed it through five markets. Our own catalogue shows it registered in all five with the same specification: **8.3 kW rated, SCOP 4.95, R32, 56 dB(A)**. From there, every euro comes from a named source.

- **🇬🇧 United Kingdom:** equipment €7,579 · installation €4,250 · VAT 0% · **the household pays €11,829**
- **🇵🇱 Poland:** equipment €8,630 · installation €5,500 · VAT 8% · **€15,260**
- **🇮🇹 Italy:** equipment €6,656 · installation €6,980 · VAT 10% · **€15,000**
- **🇫🇷 France:** equipment €6,352 · installation €9,500 · VAT 5.5% · **€16,724**
- **🇩🇪 Germany:** equipment €7,616 · installation €21,000 · VAT 19% · **€34,053**

## The heat pump costs the same. The installation does not.

Net of tax, the identical heat pump costs between €6,352 and €8,630 across the five markets. Germany sits in the middle. There is no German equipment premium on this product.

Installation is another matter: €4,250 in Britain, €21,000 in Germany — a factor of 4.9. Germany's installation cost alone exceeds the complete installed system in every other market. The reference cost analysis (Miara, heatpumpswatch.org, February 2026) itemises why: 110 technician hours against 36 for a gas boiler, a poured foundation, a three-phase connection with grid-control hardware, water treatment, balancing and paperwork. The items are real. Their sum is what a German household is asked to pay.

## Tax and the shape of support

Germany is the only market of the five at the full standard VAT rate. Britain applies zero; France 5.5%; Italy 10%; Poland 8% — unless the outdoor unit stands outside the building, in which case 23%. An air/water heat pump's outdoor unit is outside by definition.

After support, the gap does not close. A British household with the fixed £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant is left with roughly €3,100. A German household at 50% funding is left with roughly €20,000; at the maximum 70%, roughly €14,500. A percentage of the invoice rewards a higher invoice. A fixed sum does not.

## What we found that the reference could not

The reference analysis attributes part of Germany's price to premium brands dominating the market. Our register says otherwise: in Germany's 8–10 kW class, Asian manufacturers outnumber European premium brands two to one — 426 registered models against 203. The cheaper heat pump is not missing from Germany. It is registered, BAFA-listed and eligible at the same net price as anywhere else. It is a fact about what gets fitted, not about what can be bought.

## Five proposals

**Germany** — change the form of the subsidy, not the size: a fixed amount per kW removes the incentive to quote high at no extra cost; apply the reduced VAT rate every neighbour applies; standardise the grid-integration package.
**United Kingdom** — the cost side is solved; July's record 8,150 applications against 2,500 redemptions says the constraint is installer throughput, and the 0% VAT reverting to 5% in March 2027 is a scheduled price rise at the wrong moment.
**France** — the September quality tightening is right; without certification throughput it lowers the count rather than raising the floor.
**Poland** — fix one sentence: taxing the technology's normal configuration at three times the rate of the exception is drafting, not policy.
**Italy** — Europe's second-largest market has no authoritative installed-cost series; policy is set against an impression.

And our own: the same heat pump at the same price is registered in every one of these markets. Put the register in front of the household before the quote arrives. It is the cheapest intervention available and needs no legislation.
