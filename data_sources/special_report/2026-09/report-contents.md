## 1. The question

A four-person household in an existing single-family home needs roughly 8 kW of heating
capacity with domestic hot water. The same household, the same requirement, the same
heat pump — in five countries. What does it cost to get it installed, and why does the
answer differ by a factor of almost three?

The reference cost analysis is *Heat Pump Installation Costs: Germany vs. Europe*
(Dr.-Ing. Marek Miara, heatpumpswatch.org, 10 February 2026). It documents a
€10,000–€25,000 gap between Germany and its neighbours across six markets (DE, UK, FR,
NL, AT, PL) and breaks the gap into cost drivers. It does not cover Italy. It compares
market *ranges*, not one product.

**What this report adds.** We fix ONE product and follow it across five markets. Our
catalogue — the same canonical technical baseline published in every market — proves the
heat pump is identical. Published retail prices, read directly from each market's own
suppliers, show what that identical heat pump costs. The reference analysis supplies what it
costs to fit. The result separates the heat pump from everything around it.

## 2. Editorial rules

1. **One product, not a class.** Every equipment figure is for Daikin Altherma 3 R F,
   8 kW, 230-litre integrated cylinder (outdoor ERGA08 + indoor EHVX/EHVH08S23). Not an
   "8–10 kW band". Where a supplier stocks a sub-variant (6 kW vs 9 kW backup heater;
   heating-only vs heating+cooling; UK cylinder spec "SU"), the variant is recorded. The
   variants differ by a few hundred euro; the conclusions do not depend on them.
2. **Mid-market by construction.** The reference product is neither a Chinese budget
   unit nor a top-tier premium line. It is Europe's highest-volume air/water heat pump
   and is registered in all five national catalogues. This is the owner's method
   requirement and it is met by product choice, not by averaging.
3. **HeatPump DB publishes no prices.** Price fields were removed from our datasets in
   July 2026 and are guarded against. Every euro in this report comes from a named
   external source. Our catalogue contributes identity and specification only.
4. **Two sources per market, VAT status read on the page.** No heat pump price is used
   unless the supplier page states whether VAT is included, or the second source lets us
   infer it. A stale or superseded listing is dropped and the drop is recorded (§4.3).
5. **Ranges are compared at their arithmetic midpoint.** Installation cost is not
   published per product anywhere; it is a market range. Since actual sales weighting
   inside a range is unknowable, the midpoint is the only defensible single number, and
   it is labelled as such.
6. **Range-selection rule** (owner, 2026-09-08): use the primary reference's compiled
   range where it exists; exclude sources of a different scope (apartments, list-price-
   only, other capacity classes) *before* considering them outliers; exclude single
   one-off figures that disagree materially with the majority cluster; never narrow a
   range to make a table tidier.
7. **Derived arithmetic is labelled.** Installation = market total midpoint − market
   equipment-band midpoint (§5). Net-of-subsidy = HeatPump DB calculation from cited
   scheme rules (§7). Both are marked "HeatPump DB derived" wherever they appear.
8. **Availability is not market share.** Our catalogue shows what is registered and
   eligible, not what is stocked or sold. §8 is labelled accordingly.
9. **No eligibility promises.** Subsidy schemes are described by their published
   conditions; nothing states that a given household or product qualifies.
10. **The message is not diluted by exceptions.** Caveats live here and on the method
    page; the body pages carry one number per country and one sentence per finding.

## 5. Installation cost — what it takes to fit the same box

### 5.1 Method (HeatPump DB derived, per rule 7)
Installation cost is not published per product. It is derived as:
*market installed-cost midpoint (reference analysis, pre-tax) − market equipment-band
midpoint (reference analysis)*. Both inputs are the reference analysis's own compiled
ranges for 8–10 kW air/water in an existing single-family home. For Italy, absent from
the reference, installed-cost totals from Italian portals are netted of 10% IVA and the
reference-product equipment midpoint is subtracted.

| Market | Installed total (pre-tax) | Heat pump band | Installation range published | Midpoint used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | €10,000–14,000 | €6,000–9,500 → 7,750 | €2,250 – 6,250 | **€4,250** |
| Poland | €9,000–15,000 | €5,000–8,000 → 6,500 | €2,500 – 8,500 | **€5,500** |
| Italy | €10,000–20,000 incl. IVA → 9,091–18,182 net | reference product 6,656 | €2,435 – 11,526 | **€6,980** |
| France | €12,000–20,000 | €5,000–8,000 → 6,500 | €5,500 – 13,500 | **€9,500** |
| Germany | €23,000–40,000 | €9,000–12,000 → 10,500 | €12,500 – 29,500 | **€21,000** |

**How the range is carried through (added 2026-09-09, owner review).** The published
installed-cost range is carried through in full: the low end of the installation range is
*market low total − equipment-band midpoint*, the high end *market high total − equipment-band
midpoint*. The equipment reference is held at the band midpoint so that only the
installed-cost uncertainty propagates. Charts show that full range; the single figure used
for comparison is its arithmetic midpoint, labelled as such on every chart. **No chart in
this report has a zero baseline** — a heat pump does not cost €0 and neither does its
installation, so every axis starts inside the range actually observed.

**Finding 5.1 — the installation is where the money goes.** Installation runs from €4,250 in
the UK to €21,000 in Germany: a factor of **4.9**. Germany's installation cost alone
exceeds the *complete installed system* — box and fitting — in every other market.

### 5.2 Why €21,000 is credible (itemised from the reference analysis)
- Labour: ZVSHK panel (n = 1,121) — 110 technician hours per heat pump vs 36 for a gas
  boiler; end-customer rates €60–70/h → **€6,600–7,700**
- Foundation: VDI 4645 practice; Verbraucherzentrale RLP documents **€1,500–2,700**
  (RWTH Aachen: €976)
- Electrical: 400 V three-phase for 6–9 kW backup heaters; §14a EnWG control box, smart
  meter, wiring since 1 Jan 2024; VZ RLP documents **€1,800–4,000**
- Hydraulics, buffer vessel, VDI 2035 water treatment, hydraulic balancing, subsidy
  documentation, commissioning: **€5,000–8,000**
- Sum: **€15,000–22,000.** The derived €21,000 sits inside the itemised range.

Comparison markets: wall-mounting or simple substructures (≤ €500), 230 V single-phase
with optional 3 kW heater, no grid-integration obligation, shorter documented installation
times, hourly rates €45–60 (UK), €35–45 (FR), €20–30 (PL).

## 6. VAT on the installed supply

| Market | Rate on an installed heat pump | Note |
|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | **0%** | zero-rated energy-saving materials to 31 March 2027, then 5% |
| France | **5.5%** | reduced rate for energy renovation, conditions apply |
| Poland | **8%** — or 23% | 8% when the installation is within the building's structure; 23% when the unit sits outside it |
| Italy | **10%** | reduced rate for renovation, residential category A |
| Germany | **19%** | standard rate; no reduced rate applies |

**Finding 6.1 — Germany is the only one of the five at the full standard rate.**
**Finding 6.2 — Poland taxes the identical installation at two rates depending on where
the outdoor unit stands.** An air/water heat pump has an outdoor unit by definition.
The comparison uses 8% (in-envelope); at 23% the Polish household figure would be
€17,380 instead of €15,260.

## 7. The household's bill

### 7.1 Before support (the comparison figure)

| | Equipment | Installation | Pre-tax total | VAT | **Household pays** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🇬🇧 United Kingdom | €7,579 | €4,250 | €11,829 | 0% | **€11,829** |
| 🇵🇱 Poland | €8,630 | €5,500 | €14,130 | 8% | **€15,260** |
| 🇮🇹 Italy | €6,656 | €6,980 | €13,636 | 10% | **€15,000** |
| 🇫🇷 France | €6,352 | €9,500 | €15,852 | 5.5% | **€16,724** |
| 🇩🇪 Germany | €7,616 | €21,000 | €28,616 | 19% | **€34,053** |

**Finding 7.1 — Germany ÷ United Kingdom = 2.88; difference €22,224.**
Of that difference: equipment +€37, installation +€16,750, VAT +€5,437.
The heat pump explains 0.2% of the gap. The installation explains 75%. Tax explains 24%.

### 7.2 Cross-check against the only controlled same-product study
RWTH Aachen / Octopus Energy (White Paper RWTH-EBC 2025-001, DOI 10.18154/RWTH-2025-05335)
compared 8 kW air/water installations in Germany and the UK, same specification, one team:
average ~€28,000 (DE) vs ~€14,000 (UK); worked example €29,719 vs €12,095, ex VAT.
This report: €28,616 (DE, +2.2%) and €11,829 (UK, −2.2% vs the worked example).
Independent method, independent data, same answer.

### 7.3 After support (form matters more than size — HeatPump DB derived)

| Market | Scheme | Form | Applied to the figure above | Household residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | Boiler Upgrade Scheme | **fixed** £7,500 (≈ €8,745) | 11,829 − 8,745 | **≈ €3,100** (RWTH: ~€3,200) |
| Germany | BEG / KfW 458 | **proportional** 30–70% of eligible cost, cap €28,000 (since 21 Jul 2026) | 34,053 − 50% × 28,000 | **≈ €20,050** at 50%; ≈ €14,450 at 70% |
| France | MaPrimeRénov' | fixed, income-banded €4,000–9,000 | 16,724 − 6,500 (mid-band) | **≈ €10,200** |
| Poland | Czyste Powietrze | banded by income; unit rates +10% from 20 Jul 2026 | not computed — no defensible single figure | — |
| Italy | Conto Termico 3.0 / Ecobonus | direct grant ≈ €140/kW (≈ €1,160 for 8.3 kW); or 50% tax deduction over ten years | grant only: 15,000 − 1,160 | **≈ €13,840** (deduction route not a cash figure) |

**Finding 7.3 — even at Germany's maximum 70% rate, the German household's residual is
more than four times the British household's after a fixed grant.** A percentage of the
invoice rewards a higher invoice; a fixed sum does not. The reference analysis names this
as a price incentive; it is the one structural lever that acts on the number written on
the quotation without costing the treasury more.

## 8. The availability question — our own finding

The reference analysis attributes part of Germany's price level to premium European
brands (Viessmann, Vaillant, Bosch, Stiebel Eltron) dominating at €9,000–18,000 while
Asian suppliers (Daikin, Panasonic, LG, Midea) at €2,800–8,500 "play a much smaller role
in the German market than in the comparison countries." Tested against the registered
catalogue, 8–10 kW air/water:

| Market | Models registered | European premium | Asian manufacturers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | 1,360 | 203 (15%) | 426 (31%) |
| United Kingdom | 1,360 | 203 (15%) | 426 (31%) |
| France | 1,646 | 235 (14%) | 608 (37%) |
| Poland | 1,770 | 224 (13%) | 658 (37%) |
| Italy | 1,978 | 257 (13%) | 625 (32%) |

**Finding 8.1 — in the German register, Asian manufacturers outnumber European premium
brands two to one.** The reference heat pump of this report — a Daikin — is registered,
BAFA-listed and eligible in Germany at the same net price as elsewhere. The cheaper
heat pump is not missing from Germany. Germany's premium tilt is a fact about what is *sold
and fitted*, not about what can be *bought*.
Caveat (rule 8): shares of registered models, not of units sold.

## 9. What the report proposes

**🇩🇪 Germany — change the form of the subsidy, not its size.** A percentage of the
invoice makes a higher quote pay better for everyone in the chain. A fixed amount per kW,
or a fixed cap per installation, removes that incentive at zero extra cost. Second:
Germany is the only market of the five at the full VAT rate; the reduced rate every
neighbour applies to the same work is the largest single quantified item. Third: the
§14a / 400 V requirement is right in principle and re-engineered on every job in practice;
a standard domestic package would cut hours.

**🇬🇧 United Kingdom — the cost side is solved; the constraint has moved.** July's BUS
applications hit a record 8,150 while redemptions held near 2,500, as every month this
year. More grant does not move that; installer throughput does. And the 0% VAT reverts to
5% on 31 March 2027 — a scheduled ~€600 increase on a €12,000 job, arriving exactly when
volume must accelerate toward 600,000 a year.

**🇫🇷 France — quality without capacity is fewer installations, not better ones.** The
1 September tightening (European agrément required; retained gas excluded from
comprehensive renovations) points the right way. Paired with certification throughput it
raises the floor; alone it lowers the count. France already combines the second-lowest
VAT and a fixed grant — the structure is sound.

**🇵🇱 Poland — fix one sentence.** The 8% / 23% VAT split turns on whether the unit sits
inside the building envelope. An air/water heat pump's outdoor unit is outside by
definition. Taxing the technology's normal configuration at three times the rate of the
exception is a drafting artefact, not a policy choice; removing it makes Poland decisively
Europe's cheapest market for this installation.

**🇮🇹 Italy — the problem is evidence, not cost.** Europe's second-largest market has no
authoritative published installed-cost series; its price level can only be estimated from
commercial portals that disagree by a factor of two. An Assoclima or GSE cost series —
as Verbraucherzentrale publishes in Germany, MCS in the UK, ADEME in France — would let
Italian policy be set against a baseline rather than an impression.

**Our voice — make the register visible.** The same heat pump, at the same net price, is
registered in every one of these markets. The constraint is not availability; it is
visibility at the point of decision and the incentive that steers the quote. Putting the
registered catalogue in front of the household before the quote arrives is the cheapest
intervention available and needs no legislation. *Disclosure: this is also what HeatPump
DB does, and the reader should weigh the proposal knowing that.*
