# 2. Long Version — Special Report landing page

## The Complete F-Gas Rulebook for Heat Pumps

### EU Regulation 2024/573 in one document: every refrigerant, every size, every date to 2050 — and what changes in five markets.

On 1 January 2027 the EU’s revised F-gas Regulation reaches the heat pumps installers fit every day. From that date a new monoblock heat pump up to 50 kW, or a split air/water heat pump up to 12 kW, may only be placed on the EU market with a refrigerant whose global warming potential (GWP) is below 150. R32, today’s most common refrigerant, has a GWP of 675.

The rule governs placing new units on the market, not selling stock. Equipment placed on the EU market before the date may still be sold and installed; from one year after the date, the seller and the installer must be able to prove when it was placed. Banned equipment that arrives later is stopped at customs.

This report maps the whole Regulation onto one grid — 15 refrigerants, 7 sizes and builds, every date from 2025 to 2050 — and adds what each of five markets layers on top.

### One rulebook, five markets

- **Germany** — EU dates apply. From 1 January 2028 BEG funds only heat pumps with natural refrigerants; the 5-point efficiency bonus ended on 21 July 2026.
- **United Kingdom** — Great Britain keeps its own F-gas law and has no 2027 heat-pump ban. Northern Ireland follows the EU dates.
- **France** — EU dates apply. The CEE heat-pump sheet sets no refrigerant criterion; the bonus depends on the ADEME agrément.
- **Poland** — EU dates apply. Czyste Powietrze sets no refrigerant criterion; the model must be on the Lista ZUM.
- **Italy** — EU dates apply. Conto Termico 3.0 sets no GWP condition for air/water heat pumps.

### 2027 is the first big step, not the last

The Regulation closes the market in stages. In 2027 refrigerants with GWP 150 or more leave monoblocks up to 50 kW and air/water splits up to 12 kW. In 2029 splits above 12 kW lose GWP 750 or more; in 2030 monoblocks above 50 kW lose GWP 150 or more; in 2033 splits above 12 kW lose GWP 150 or more.

Small units then leave fluorinated gases altogether: monoblocks up to 12 kW from 2032, splits up to 12 kW from 2035. From 2035 a new heat pump up to 12 kW can only use a natural refrigerant such as R290 propane or R744 CO₂, unless safety rules at the site require otherwise.

### What it means for R32

R32 does not disappear overnight. After 2026 it stays available for new splits above 12 kW until the end of 2032 and for new monoblocks above 50 kW until the end of 2029. Units already placed on the market can be sold and installed after those dates.

In the HeatPump DB European reference catalogue, 56% of residential models use a refrigerant with GWP 150 or more, mostly R32 and R410A; 44% already use R290.

### Every date has a safety exemption

Each prohibition allows an exception where safety requirements at the site of operation rule out the alternative; for monoblocks the limit then becomes GWP 750. Such equipment must be labelled for that use, and the operator must keep the evidence for five years.

Servicing follows its own rule: since 1 January 2026, new refrigerant with GWP 2,500 or more may not be used to service heat pumps. R410A, at 2,088, is below that line.

### Same EU dates, different money

The bans apply directly in every EU member state. What differs is the subsidy: Germany ties BEG funding to natural refrigerants from 2028, while France, Poland and Italy set no refrigerant condition for air/water heat pumps.

Great Britain is the exception in law: it has not adopted the EU’s 2027 heat-pump ban, and R32 monoblocks stay on sale there after 2026.

# 3. Wording rules for derivatives (LinkedIn, newsletters, sales)

- Say "may not be placed on the EU market as a new unit", never "R32 is banned".
- Always add the stock rule: units placed before the date may still be sold and installed; from one year after the date, with proof of when they were placed.
- Germany: natural refrigerants are a BEG condition from **1 January 2028** (not 2026).
- Great Britain has no 2027 heat-pump ban; Northern Ireland follows the EU.
- Links carry `?ref=` (e.g. `?ref=li`).
